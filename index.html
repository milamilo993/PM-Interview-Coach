TL;DR

Bislab is a small, Oslo-founded (2022) fintech building an API-first, AI-native credit risk and business-data platform — credit ratings, fraud/AML detection, transaction analysis, continuous monitoring — sold to banks, insurers, and debt-collection firms. Backed by ~€1.7M pre-seed (Nov 2024) plus NOK 50M (Aug 2025, led by ProFounders and Futurum Ventures), live in Norway, Sweden, and Finland, with stated plans to expand "soon beyond" the Nordics — the literal mandate behind this role. It positions itself as a modern, developer-friendly challenger to legacy credit bureaus (UC/Enento, Bisnode/D&B, Creditsafe, CRIF). The timing case is concrete: EU AI Act high-risk rules for credit-scoring AI take effect August 2, 2026 — essentially now.

1. What They Do

The Product

  • API-based SaaS: company & personal credit ratings, company data, fraud/AML checks, transaction analysis, continuous entity change-monitoring
  • Newer positioning adds AI agents for workflow automation
  • Holds credit-rating-agency licenses in each market it operates in

Customers

  • Banking, finance, insurance, debt collection
  • Named clients: Intrum, Tietoevry, BankID, Fiken, Eika Forsikring; public contract with Norway's Tax Administration
  • ~50 clients reported as of late 2024, growing since

How the Data & AI Work

Tailored scorecard models (separate for personal vs. corporate credit) rather than one generic bureau score, combining registry data — e.g. Norway's Gjeldsregisteret (mandatory national debt register) — with real-time/open-banking data via a 2024 Neonomics partnership. Framed as "AI-native": AI built into the modeling from the start, not bolted onto a legacy statistical model. Bislab's COO has publicly claimed a model ROC score above 90 vs. ~80 considered "very high quality" market-wide — a self-reported marketing figure, not independently verified.

Bislab's Claimed Differentiation

  • API-first vs. sales-led: markets a developer-friendly integration path (claims ~3–4 hour typical integration) against bureaus' longer enterprise sales/onboarding cycles
  • Real-time vs. periodic: open-banking + registry-API data pitched as an alternative to "outdated datasets and manual processes"

Honest Caveat

Most differentiation claims above come from Bislab's own marketing and partner press releases, not third-party benchmarking. There's no independent data on accuracy, coverage breadth, or pricing versus UC, Creditsafe, or CRIF. Fine to repeat the story in conversation — just don't present it as externally verified fact.

3. Why This Is Interesting to Solve Right Now

EU AI Act — Immediate

Credit-scoring AI becomes classified high-risk under Annex III(5)(b) from Aug 2, 2026 — days away. New obligations: data governance, human oversight, fundamental rights impact assessments, 10-year record retention. Legacy bureaus must retrofit; a recently-built platform can architect for it natively.

Open Banking Maturity

Real-time account/transaction data is now programmatically accessible (see Neonomics partnership) — a structural shift from periodic bureau snapshots to continuous signal, not just a nice-to-have feature.

Registry Digitization

Norway's Gjeldsregisteret (mandatory bank check, ~NOK 159.5bn registered unsecured debt) is modern and API-accessible — infrastructure that used to require expensive incumbent relationships now lowers the barrier for smaller entrants.

Incumbent Inertia + Cross-Border Timing

UC/Enento, Bisnode/D&B, and CRIF are large, consolidated, sales-led organizations on older architecture — the classic setup where an API-first, engineering-led buying motion wins share, especially with digital-native lenders wanting instant decisions inside their own product flows (the same dynamic that's already helped Creditsafe win share among digital lenders like Qred and Froda). As Bislab expands beyond Norway/Sweden/Finland, each new country brings its own registry-access landscape, licensing regime, and incumbent structure — the "why now" argument has to be re-proven market by market, which is exactly what this role is scoped to do.
